      إسناد

      Our nameلماذا إسناد

      Named after the science of trust

      Isnād comes from an Arabic root meaning to lean on, or to support. In hadith scholarship it is the chain of people through whom a saying reached us. One narrator heard it from another, all the way back to the Prophet ﷺ.

      The scholars studied every person in that chain. Was each narrator honest? Did they remember well? Had they really met the one they quoted? A report was only as strong as its weakest link. We took the name because we want to be held to that standard.

      Long before al-Bukhārī, ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak put it this way:

      الإِسْنَادُ مِنَ الدِّينِ، وَلَوْلَا الإِسْنَادُ لَقَالَ مَنْ شَاءَ مَا شَاءَ

      “The isnad is part of the religion. Were it not for the isnad, anyone could say whatever they wished.”

      ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak (d. 181 AH), in the introduction to Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim

      Five links in every story

      Hadith scholars graded a report ṣaḥīḥ, sound, only when five conditions held. We put every story through the same five before we publish it. Tap a link to break it and see what happens to the grade.
